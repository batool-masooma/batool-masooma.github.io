I follow nitrogen through 170 years of European farming.

I work at the Helmholtz Centre for Environmental Research (UFZ) in Leipzig, Germany. I reconstruct nitrogen and phosphorus surpluses for Europe back to 1850, and use them to test whether EU policies are enough to cut nutrient pollution. My datasets are open, and I like it when other people put them to use.

My path to Europe started with a fully funded Netherlands Fellowship Programme scholarship for an MSc at Wageningen University & Research.

Which nitrogen type is your region?

With my colleagues, I grouped 393 European regions by how their nitrogen inputs, outputs and land use changed between 1850 and 2019. Four types came out of it. Pick one to read about it.

The EU wants to halve nutrient losses by 2030 and use at least 20% less fertilizer. In our scenarios, a 20% fertilizer cut on its own lowers agricultural nitrogen surplus by only

10–16%

or 16–28% if technologies and management improve. Each type of region needs its own strategy. Read the paper
